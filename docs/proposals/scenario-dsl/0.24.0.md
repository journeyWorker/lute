## 1. The clock

A schema MAY declare one clock:

```yaml
clock:
  day: run.day            # number path, owner: engine
  slot: run.slot          # optional: enum path, owner: engine
  slots: [morning, afternoon, night]   # with `slot`: the clock order of the slot enum's members
  raise: slotStart        # optional: one occasion, or a map (below)
  week:                   # optional
    length: 7
    first: 1              # the weekday index of day 1 (0-based)
    labels: [Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu, Fri, Sat, Sun]
```

- `day` and `slot` MUST be declared `owner: engine` (else `E-CLOCK-DECL`).
- `slots` MUST list exactly the slot enum's members, each once (else
  `E-CLOCK-DECL`), in any order: `slots`, not the enum, sets the clock's
  order.
- `slot` and `slots` go together. A clock without them counts whole days: a
  day is its one slot, a position is its day (`day 3`), and `once: slot`
  means `once: day`. (A one-member slot enum is no longer needed.)
- `raise` is one occasion — raised after every advance, where the clock
  stops — or a map naming the occasion for each moment, each optional:
  `raise: { slot: slotStart, dayStart: dayStart, dayEnd: dayEnd }`. `slot`
  is the one-occasion form. `dayEnd` is raised at every midnight an advance
  crosses, before the crossing, with the day not yet advanced; `dayStart`
  right after it, at the next day's first slot. Each midnight is a stop:
  the clock moves there, the quests settle, then the occasion is raised.
  None of the three is raised by an `engine:` write to the clock paths.
- Reserved read-only paths: `clock.weekday` (number, `0..length-1`),
  `clock.weekdayLabel` (string, renderable), `clock.index` (number,
  `(day-1)*len(slots) + slotIndex`, monotone; `day-1` without slots).
  Content reads them anywhere a CEL condition or interpolation is legal.
- Scene beats accept `once: day` / `once: slot` (spent until the day / slot
  changes); entries accept `once="day"|"slot"`.
- `lute play` step `advance: slot | day | <n slots>`: moves the clock forward
  (never backward; wraps slots into the next day), settles quests, and raises
  `raise`'s `slot` occasion once, where it stops — never at the slots it
  passes. `advance: day` lands on the next day's first slot, whatever slot
  it starts from. `advance: <n>` never skips a day's close: with `dayEnd`
  declared it stops at each day's last slot to raise it; `advance: day`
  raises `dayEnd` where the clock stands (the rest of the day is skipped). An
  `advance:` step MAY carry `engine:` writes: they land where the clock
  arrives — after every `dayEnd` / `dayStart` stop, before the final settle
  and `slot` raise — so a day's close reads the day it closes. The step's
  `expect.presented` lists what every raise of the step presented, in
  order; `winner` / `offered` / `notOffered` judge the final raise. An
  `occasion:` step raising the clock's own `dayEnd` / `dayStart` is noted
  (the next advance raises it again). An `engine:` step that moves
  `clock.index` backward is a usage error. Play scripts MAY `include: <file>`
  steps.
- `lute calendar --axis clock[=d1..d2]` expands to day × slot in order;
  `--occasion O@clock.day` (or `O@<day path>`, with `,clock.slot=<slot>` /
  `,<slot path>=<slot>`) evaluates `O` once per day at one slot.

Independent pieces shipped alongside: an enum member MAY carry a display
label (`enums: { weekday: { members: [...], labels: {...} } }`) that
interpolation renders; integer `%` joins the CEL profile (both operands must
be integers; a non-integer is `E-CEL-TYPE`); `::set{… when="…"}` applies the
write only when the condition holds.

## 2. Quest structure

- `<quest activate="accept">` on a child quest: it does NOT activate with its
  parent; it waits for `::accept` (then activates only while the parent is
  active). `::accept` of a child that auto-activates is `E-ACCEPT-TARGET`.
- `<quest complete="any">` on a parent: it completes when **any** required
  child completes; the other still-open children fail with
  `failedBy: superseded`. Default `complete="all"`.
- Reserved read-only `quest.<id>.failedBy` (`unset|fail|by|until|cascade|superseded`)
  and `quest.<id>.objectives.<o>.failed` (bool).
- An occasion MAY declare `judge: before`: its `on=` objectives are judged
  (and the quests settled) before its beats are presented (default `after`).
  The `<on>` handler bodies that raise answers — the same-named event's and
  the lifecycle handlers of the quests it settles — still run after the
  beats.
- `::accept{quest="…" at="nextRun"}` queues the acceptance; it applies after
  the next `newRun` reset (run-tier quests taken on a hub between runs).
- `<on event="E" target="…">` fires only when the same-named occasion is
  raised for that target.
- A bundle beat is a legal `after:` predecessor (`visited('<doc>.<beat>')`,
  `after: visited(...)` on quests); an accept-driven quest without `after=`
  is anchored at every document that `::accept`s it.
- `W-QUEST-NEVER-ACCEPTED` (check-project): an accept-driven quest no
  `::accept` names and no `accepts:` mock could reach.

### 2.1 Deadlines

`by=` is judged at **every settle**, for every objective, whether or not it
has `on=` (reverses 0.23.1's raise-only rule for `on=` objectives: a deadline
is a moment, not a place). `done` still wins a tie in the same settle, and
in the step that raises an `on=` objective's occasion its `by` waits for the
raise: `done` is judged first, then `by`. A `by` that comes true in any other
step fails the objective there. The old raise-only behaviour is `until=`:
judged only when the objective's occasion (and target) is raised, after
`done`.

`W-DEADLINE-BEFORE-DONE`: an `on=` objective with `by=` and no `until=` whose
`done` provably implies `by` — `by` comes true, and fails the objective, at a
settle before the occasion ever judges `done` (unless both happen in the step
that raises it); the message suggests `until=` with the same condition.

## 3. Entities

- `entities: { companion: { subsetOf: person, members: [isolde, corvin] } }`:
  every member MUST be a member of the parent kind; a sub-kind is legal
  wherever a kind is (relation args, occasion target domains) and an argument
  of kind `companion` is also of kind `person`.
- Entity-indexed state: `run.approval: { type: number, default: 0, per: companion }`
  declares `run.approval.<member>` for every member. In a rule `cel()` guard,
  `run.approval[P]` reads the member bound to `P` by a positive atom; anywhere
  else members are addressed by name. An index over a non-closed kind is
  `E-STATE-DECL`.

## 4. Casts and parties

- A cast entry MAY declare `present: "<condition>"`, `emotions: [...]` and
  `assume: true`. `W-CAST-ABSENT`: a line by that speaker where the
  conjunction of its enclosing guards (line `when=`, match arms, choice
  guards, beat `when:`) does not imply `present` (decided by the condition
  decider and the fact Must set). `emotion=` outside the speaker's
  `emotions:` is `E-BAD-ENUM`.
  - Also assumed: an entry's own `entry.<id>.everRead`/`.read` inside its
    body; in a quest `<on>` handler, the quest's state at that event
    (`active`; `complete`/`failed` for `questComplete`/`questFailed`) and
    each `start` conjunct that stays true once it held
    (`entry.<id>.everRead`, `visited('…')`); under `check-project`, the
    spend flag of every always-eligible `once` beat ranked above on the
    same ladder (the beat below wins only after it).
    Also under `check-project`, a beat, entry or bundle beat presented at
    most once per run starts from `!holds(F)` for each ground fact `F`
    only it asserts. Nothing else in the root asserts `F`, no seed names
    it, and its relation is `tier: run` (or `tier: user` in a `once: user`
    beat), neither derived nor reserved. Its own `::assert{F}` ends that on
    the path.
  - A guard `holds(A)` of a derived relation implies its rules' bodies
    (head bound; a non-ground literal dropped). Where every unifying rule
    is ground once bound, `holds(A)` IS the disjunction of the bodies on
    both sides of the implication — a `cel()`-only schedule stands for its
    guards, as in `W-BEAT-PRIORITY-TIE`.
  - A write stops a guard counting only when it can falsify it on that
    path: a `::set` of a path it (or a rule `cel()` it reads through)
    reads; an `::assert`/`::retract` of a unifiable atom, directly or through
    a rule where the written relation is a premise, moving it the wrong
    way. Sibling choices and match arms are separate paths; a guard
    survives a branch when it survives every arm.
  - A `{vo}` line is exempt (a voice-over does not put its speaker in the
    room); an `{os}` line is not (the speaker is there, out of frame).
  - `assume: true` reads every negated `holds` of an engine-`reserved`
    relation in `present` (directly or through a rule) as true. The checker
    does not know when the engine writes a reserved fact, so without it a
    `present` over one is satisfied only by a guard at each line or beat;
    with it, content after the engine event (a companion's line after a
    lost battle) needs its own guard.
- `::auto{character}` and `::camera{focus}` are checked against the cast
  (`E-CAST-UNKNOWN`), like speakers.
- `::clear` (staging leaf): every character on stage exits.
- Component `effects: true`: the body may `::set` / `::assert` / `::retract`;
  writes are checked at each `::use` site against the host's schema. A
  component param of type `speaker` takes a cast id and renders the cast
  name in `{{@p}}`.
- Interpolation MAY carry a format hint: `{{user.deaths:ordinal}}`; the IR
  placeholder gains `format`, the engine renders it (the reference runner
  renders English ordinals).

## 5. Bridges in play and test

Play scripts accept `bridges: { <tag>: [ {<field>: value}, … ] }` (top level,
consumed in order) and per step. Scenario tests and trace mocks accept the
same `bridges:` key. An unanswered bridge halts **before** any default arm is
walked (play) and is unresolved (trace/test), never silently defaulted.

## 6. Entries

- On an untargeted occasion, an entry's `target=` is metadata (no longer
  `E-BEAT-ATTR`).
- A guard that assumes `entry.X.read` (resp. `everRead` for user-tier facts)
  adds the facts X's body asserts on every path to the Must set.

## 8. Decisions

| # | Decision | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **D-A** | one minor for model + fixes | fewer releases to track; fixes and the features they unblock ship together |
| **D-B** | the clock is declared on existing engine-owned paths | keeps state the single source; the clock adds meaning (order, weekday, once) not storage |
| **D-C** | `by=` is always a moment; `until=` is the place-bound form | round 3's VN missed its festival because the deadline waited for a visit |
| **D-D** | `complete="any"` fails the rest as superseded | the RPG author's hand-written cascade was the bug |
| **D-E** | presence is a cast condition, not a new tier | party membership is ordinary state/facts; the checker only needs to know which condition a speaker needs |

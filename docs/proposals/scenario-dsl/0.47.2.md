# Lute 0.47.2 — fix patch

This is the normative 0.47.2 contract, a patch release on the 0.47 contract
line. The words **MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **SHOULD** and **MAY** have their RFC
2119 meanings. Inputs are issues #137 and #138, both pre-existing player
defects (same on 0.47.0 and 0.47.1).

## 1. Scope and version contract

The toolchain, language, IR, Event/snapshot stamps, player packages and
launcher packages read `0.47.2`. Schema filenames and bodies stay on the
`0.47` line. Only `@lute-lang/player` and `@lute-lang/player-web` change
behaviour.

| Axis | 0.47.2 rule |
|---|---|
| Toolchain | Cargo workspace, `lute version` and all seven npm packages stamp `0.47.2`. |
| Language | `luteVersion` stamps become `0.47.2`; no syntax or semantic change. |
| IR | `irVersion` stamps become `0.47.2`; no field or meaning change. |
| Event/snapshot | Stamps become `0.47.2`; shapes and schema bodies unchanged. |
| Player bindings | `bindingsVersion` stays `"0.47"`; schema body and generated TypeScript unchanged. |
| Player | Save/profile format stays `"0.47"`. Every cue of a batch starts (§2); a restore does not wait for music to start, and music starts once audio unlocks (§3). |

The release MUST NOT alter the bodies of
`schemas/lute-{ir,events,snapshot,player-bindings}-0.47.schema.json`,
`packages/runtime/src/generated/**` or `packages/player/src/bindings/schema.ts`.
Every command output captured by `scripts/cli-snapshot.py` MUST be identical
to 0.47.1 after version-stamp normalization (no Rust behaviour changes).

## 2. Every cue of a batch starts (#137)

0.40.0 §6.3: the scheduler runs a batch's cues in order; a cue it does not
wait for (no `wait`, not a line or sequence; and every timeline clip) runs on
a fiber forked into the batch scope, and stage cues are joined before the
batch ends. Before 0.47.2 the batch scope could close before such a fiber
had run at all: nothing yields between `scheduler.play` returning and the
scope closing unless an autosave is written — so with `autosave` `false` or
`"presentation"` a camera, vfx or sfx cue that was the last before a choice
was never started, and in every mode the same happened before an `idle`
await (no autosave there) and to a one-shot cue still in its `delay` when
the batch ended.

- `CueScheduler.play` MUST NOT complete before every cue it forked — each
  non-waiting cue and each timeline clip, counted per clip — has
  **started**: its `delay` (plus timeline `at`) elapsed, `CueStarted`
  published and its `execute` effect begun running — or, in skip mode, its
  `complete` effect begun running. A cue whose fiber exits before starting
  (interrupted, or a defect) also releases the wait, so a never-started cue
  cannot hang the batch. This holds whatever the autosave mode and whatever
  await follows the batch.
- A forked cue with a `delay` therefore holds the batch until its delay has
  elapsed; it does not wait for the cue to finish. A cue still in its delay
  (or `at`) when the mode becomes `skip` is `complete`d at once, which counts
  as started (0.40.0 §6.3 item 5: nothing waits on the clock in skip).
- Unchanged: stage cues are still joined before the batch ends; one-shot cues
  (sfx, vfx, camera) are not joined (0.40.0 §6.3 item 4), and the batch scope
  still interrupts a started one-shot cue's `execute` when the batch ends, as
  since 0.40 (a started camera transition runs on the camera's own fiber and
  plays out, 0.47.1 §4). Interrupting a batch (load, rollback, title) still
  interrupts its cues, started or not.
- Regression tests (player, over a compiled fixture through
  `@lute-lang/runtime`): a `::camera` and a `::sfx` as the last records
  before a `<choice>`; with `autosave: false` and with `"presentation"` the
  presenter receives the camera cue and the audio port the sfx play before
  the choice is shown; with the default autosave, a `::sfx` as the last record
  before an `idle` await is played. Scheduler: a delayed one-shot cue then a
  line, switched to skip during the delay, settles without the clock
  advancing.

## 3. A load completes while audio is locked (#138)

Before 0.47.2, restoring the stage (load, host `restore`, rollback) awaited
`AudioPort.play("music", …)` for the stage music (0.41.0 §7.7). player-web's
`PixiAudio` holds every play until Web Audio is unlocked by a user gesture,
so a load issued before any gesture (the host API: `act({ type: "load" })`,
`restore`) never reached the restored await.

- **Director.** Restoring the stage MUST NOT wait for the stage music: the
  music call (`play` the stage music, or `stop`) begins running before the
  restored await is presented, on a fiber forked in the player scope, and is
  not awaited (nor interrupted by a later restore). The presenter restore
  still completes before the await is presented. This holds for every
  `AudioPort`; since the restored call is no longer serialized before the
  next batch, an `AudioPort` MUST let a later music `play`/`stop` win over an
  earlier music `play` still in progress (stated in the `AudioPort` docs).
- **player-web music order.** In `PixiAudio` a music `play` or `stop`
  supersedes every earlier music `play` still in progress (waiting for the
  unlock, its file, or the backend). A superseded play completes at once,
  successfully, without starting; if its backend play resolves after it was
  superseded, that playback is stopped at once and never recorded. Music
  already playing is replaced as before (crossfade over the new play's
  `fade`; the same file without `restart` only changes volume). So while
  audio is locked only the latest music request survives, and it starts when
  audio unlocks: the title music requested at mount is superseded by a loaded
  stage's music, or by its silence. sfx and voice are unchanged.
- Browser test (player-web, over a compiled bundle, no mock): play with
  device input and save; mount again over the same storage (audio locked);
  load with no user gesture (host `act({ type: "load" })`); the restored
  await is reached with no music playing; a click then starts the stage's
  music, read from real `@pixi/sound` state, and the title music never
  starts. Unit (player-web): two music plays before unlock both complete and
  only the second starts.

## 4. Testing

Each fix has a regression test that fails on 0.47.1. Player / player-web
behaviour is tested against real runtime output (compiled fixtures, the
browser suite over compiled bundles), never a hand-written view or record.

## 5. Out of scope

#139 (a literal `{` directly before a marker); everything else.

## 6. Breaking changes and migration

| Class | Change | Migration |
|---|---|---|
| Player | A batch settles only after each of its non-waiting cues and timeline clips started; a delayed one holds the batch for its delay (in every autosave mode; skip completes it at once). | None. |
| Player | A restore no longer waits for the stage music to start. | A custom `AudioPort` lets a later music `play`/`stop` win over an earlier music `play` still in progress. |
| player-web | A music `play`/`stop` supersedes earlier music plays still in progress; while locked only the latest survives and starts at unlock. | None. |
| Release | All axes `0.47.2`; schemas, `bindingsVersion` and save format stay on `0.47`. | None. |
